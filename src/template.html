1. Discover volumes

Choose Open folder to include all its images and subfolders, or use Files / ZIP for selected files and archives. Chrome needs you to select the folder; choosing one file does not grant access to its neighbors. Dropping folders is also supported.

Volumes group by subfolder, filename prefix (including separators), numeric suffix length, and image type. For example, PMSpecimen1O_000000.tif and PMSpecimen1O000.tif form separate six-digit and three-digit sequences. Different names and subfolders remain separate. Use the volume selector above the viewer to switch. Put unrelated acquisitions that share the same naming pattern in separate subfolders.

2. Link the fundus and B-scan

For the 1008 × 596 Heidelberg composite layout, the left 496 × 496 region is the fundus and the right 512 × 496 region is the B-scan. The footer is excluded. The app detects the green acquisition rectangle and arrow in each frame to place the active B-scan on the fundus. Click within the acquisition rectangle to select the nearest loaded scan. The fundus displayed is the original panel from the nearest loaded frame; teal marks the current or interpolated plane trace. If no arrow is detected, the source fundus is displayed without an inferred scan position.

Use Expand for a larger navigator. Switch Fundus to En face for the reconstructed mean / maximum projection or current depth section. A fundus photo and an en face intensity projection are different images. Oblique traces use the detected acquisition grid; they are not additional acquired scans.

3. Manipulate the section

Move the scan slider, select an acquired thumbnail, or use ← / →. Yaw rotates the plane and tilt moves toward an en face section. Drag the 3D view to rotate and scroll to zoom. R resets the plane.

4. Calibration and export

Image scale markers are detected on import. Clear L markers with confidently recognized integer µm / mm / nm labels set X/Z spacing automatically. Review the detected marker and edit its label or pixel lengths when necessary. Scan interval Y is separate. Enter all three acquisition spacings in µm for physical 3D geometry. X / Z refer to original cropped pixels and Y to adjacent original scan intervals. With only X/Z known, measurements inside a native B-scan use µm, and the image aspect follows X/Z calibration. Measurements crossing an unknown axis require its spacing. The scan axis stays expanded until Y is known. TIFF DPI is ignored. Use Export on the B-scan to save a PNG with optional calibrated bars, measurements and an intensity legend, alone or paired with its source fundus and linked scan trace. Unknown scale is labeled and never guessed. Burned-in marks in the original image remain even if the overlay is disabled. Video offers a 360° orbit or a sweep through the scan axis with the volume, native B-scan, and optional fundus. Videos retain red–cyan stereo when enabled and save as seekable WebM. Chrome uses frame-by-frame WebCodecs encoding where supported, preserving the chosen duration and frame rate. Export time depends on rendering speed. A recorder fallback also writes a seekable clip with the selected duration and reports the captured frame rate. Keep the tab visible while creating a video. Cancel discards the video and restores your view. Download app saves a self-contained HTML with no example dataset or imported images.

Intensity color maps

Choose Clinical spectrum to display weak-to-strong intensity as blue, cyan, green, yellow, orange and red, as in the supplied example. Inferno, Viridis and grayscale are also available. Black / white levels and gamma set the input display range, and Color strength blends the palette with grayscale. The color legend follows these settings in the viewer and exports. Color maps change display only; they do not segment tissue or compute thickness.

Red–cyan stereo

Enable Red–cyan 3D glasses under Rendering. The two eyes use separate off-axis camera views, converging at the orbit center. Use a red left lens and cyan right lens; Swap eyes reverses the mapping. Depth strength adjusts eye separation, and zero returns to a single viewpoint. Volume, maximum intensity and plane-only modes all support stereo; PNG export preserves it. Stereo is a display aid and adds no acquired anatomical information.

Lesion volume analysis

Choose Lesions on the B-scan to outline laser CNV or another injury. The editor uses the original cropped source pixels and acquired scans, independent of display reconstruction quality. Click around a lesion and finish the polygon, then drag vertices to adjust it. Add contours on other scans and mark a scan with no lesion as Absent at each end when possible. Each named lesion is measured separately; overlapping lesions are not combined.

Positive X, Y and Z spacing is required for µm³. Y is the center-to-center distance between adjacent discovered scans in file order; filename numbers establish order, not physical distance. You can enter the measured first-to-last scan-center range to calculate Y spacing. Numbering gaps or missing original scans require the correct spacing or a complete sequence. With only X/Z, cross-sectional areas are available.

Volume is the trapezoidal integral Σ (Aᵢ + Aᵢ₊₁) / 2 × ΔY between the first and last annotated scan centers, with linear area interpolation across gaps and no extrapolation beyond them. Turn off interpolation to require every scan in that range to be reviewed. Nonzero end contours are labeled partial extent. Save ROIs to resume later; summary and per-section CSV exports include calibration, bounds, gaps and the calculation method. This measures the tissue you outline on structural OCT; it does not identify CNV automatically or isolate perfused vessels.

Memory and input formats

Only the selected volume is decoded; switching releases the previous volume. Large sequences are evenly sampled to a maximum of 512 files and approximately 96 MB decoded source pixels, with a visible loaded / discovered count. The full file catalog stays available. This samples frames, so fine structures between loaded scans are not recovered. For full resolution, place a smaller contiguous scan range in its own folder.

Classic strip TIFF supports unsigned 8 / 16-bit grayscale or RGB, uncompressed, PackBits, LZW, and Deflate; PNG and JPEG are also supported. Multi-page TIFF is supported within the source memory budget. 3D uses WebGL 2 with a CPU fallback. No registration, motion correction, segmentation, or anatomical surface inference is applied. Screen-export scale marks within the B-scan crop remain part of the display data.
